Welcome! My name is Ethan Butz and I'm happy to welcome you to my portfolio website. Along the top you can find links to my [CV](/cv/), a few of the [projects](/projects/) I've worked on, my conference [posters](/posters/), and my [teaching](/teaching/) experience. At the bottom of the page, you can find ways to contact me or judge my taste in movies and games. 

I was born in Beijing, China to an American family working as Christian missionaries. My sister and I were raised overseas in Beijing and Singapore until 2012 when we moved to Orlando, Florida. After finishing high school at the International Community School in Winter Park, I started my college education at the [University of Florida](ufl.edu) in Gainesville pursuing an dual degree in Mechanical and Aerospace Engineering. In 2023, I graduated with both degrees and began the PhD program at U.F. to pursue a doctorate in Aerospace Engineering at the [Precision Space Systems Laboratory](https://pssl.mae.ufl.edu/) under [Dr. John W. Conklin](https://scholar.google.com/citations?user=C-oDA-AAAAAJ&hl=en). In 2025, I was awarded my Master's of Science in Aerospace Engineering, and I hope to graduate in 2028 with my doctorate. 

At PSSL, my focus has been on mechanical and circuit design, ground testing and metrology of space components, payload assembly and integration, and precision manufacturing. My primary focus as been on the [GRATTIS](/projects/GRATTIS/) project, a [NASA ESTO](https://esto.nasa.gov/project-selections-for-invest-23/) satellite mission to confirm performance of a novel ultra-sensitive electrostatic accelerometer for space-based geodesy, the study of gravity. Early at PSSL on the electronics team, I did circuit board layout and electronics sensitivity training for the accelerometer electrode signal stability which provided a strong basis of understanding for the sensor design. The next summer, I was asked to manufacture a cubic test mass for our ground testing equipment. I was able to manufacture four specimens that were perfectly cubic to within 10 micrometers. Since then, I've been the primary researcher in charge of flight component, ground testing, and payload metrology to ensure all parts maintain positioning necessary to meet mission objectives. The sensor packages specifically have a relative positional knowledge requirement of less than 10 micrometers across a 22 centermeter separation, maintained throughout the mission lifetime, which will make up a majority of my dissertation. More detail on the GRATTIS mission can be found [here](/projects/GRATTIS/)!

Outside of my work as a researcher, I enjoy building things at home, 3D printing in resin and plastic, cooking, and spending time with friends and family. I've also been involved in my church and bible study, building up my faith, discipling others, and working to glorify God in all I do. It was there I met my beautiful fiancée Amy, to whom I am trilled to be marrying in October.
